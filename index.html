5 Gear Contact Pattern Guide

Apply a thin coat of marking compound (gear blue or similar) to 6–8 ring gear teeth. Rotate the ring gear by hand with load resistance on both directions. Examine the contact pattern. A correct pattern means your pinion depth is set properly.

CORRECT

Ideal Contact Pattern

Pattern is centered on the tooth, slightly toward the toe. Occupies roughly the center 60–70% of tooth width and height. Good noise and wear characteristics.

No adjustment needed. Assembly is correct.
PINION TOO DEEP

Pattern Toward Toe (Face)

Pattern concentrated at the toe (narrow end) and/or top of the tooth face. Pinion is pushed too far into the ring gear.

Fix: Remove pinion shim to pull the pinion back out (decrease depth).
PINION TOO SHALLOW

Pattern Toward Heel (Flank)

Pattern at the heel (wide end) and/or bottom of the tooth flank. Pinion is not pushed deep enough into the ring gear.

Fix: Add pinion shim to push the pinion deeper (increase depth).
BACKLASH EFFECT

Drive vs. Coast Pattern Shift

Drive and coast patterns are separated top-to-bottom (not side-to-side). This is controlled by backlash, not pinion depth.

Fix: Adjust backlash (move shims side-to-side) until patterns are symmetrical around tooth center.
Important reminder: Specs above are typical ranges. Always verify pinion bearing preload, backlash, and torque specs against your specific axle service manual. Pinion etched markings (+/−) modify the standard mounting distance — a positive etching means the pinion wants to be that much further from the centerline; a negative etching means closer. Factor this into your target marking value.
